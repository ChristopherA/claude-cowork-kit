# Review the week

Once a week, look back before planning forward. A week reviewed in a few honest lines shows what a day cannot: the thing that has slipped three weeks running, the priority that has gone quiet, the kind of week that leaves the reader flat. This skill asks a few questions, writes a dated entry to a reviews doc, lists what was carried forward from the last review and not touched, and proposes the change to `priorities.md` the week earned.

## Before starting

Read `priorities.md` and the last two or three entries in `reviews.md`, a project doc created the first time this runs, so the review compares against something. Both are project docs, so this works from the phone. If a plan for the week was handed back by the planning skill, read it too; the review measures the week against the plan the reader chose, not against an ideal.

## The questions

Conversational, one or two at a time, in the reader's words, skipping what they have said:

1. **What got done?** From the plan and beyond it. Keep the reader's words where they are concrete.
2. **What slipped?** What was planned and did not happen, and what took its place. Not why yet.
3. **What keeps being avoided?** The item that has moved from week to week. Name it if the last reviews show it; the reader confirms or corrects.
4. **What ate the week?** The unplanned thing, the meeting that ran over, the day lost. Once, not as a complaint.
5. **Energy.** How the week felt: flat, steady, over full. One word or a line.

## The entry and the carry-forward

Draft a dated entry: done, slipped, avoided, what ate the week, energy, in short lines. Under it, the carry-forward: every item from the last review's slipped and avoided lines that did not come up this week, with the date it first appeared, so an item three reviews old is visibly three reviews old. The week itself cannot tell you what did not happen; the last review can. Show the entry, wait for a yes, write it to `reviews.md`, read it back, and confirm.

## What moved

Say what moved against the last few reviews, specifically and in the entries' own words: a kind of item that slips every week, an avoided thing that finally happened, energy trending one way. Say it plainly in either direction; do not manufacture progress and do not add a lesson. If the reviews show the priorities have changed, a listed priority quiet for a month, an unlisted thing eating every week, propose the edit to `priorities.md`: show the new text, wait for a yes, write it, read it back. A no or silence leaves it as it was.

## Ending

Report the entry written, the carry-forward count, the one or two things that moved, and whether `priorities.md` changed. End with one word for completeness: full (entry written and compared), partial (entry drafted but not written, with why), or minimal (stopped early). Then say that the planning skill is the next step, if the reader wants to plan the coming week now.

## What this skill does not do

It does not plan the week; the planning skill does. It does not change `priorities.md` without its own yes. It does not send anything or change a calendar. It does not judge the week; it records it and says what moved.

## Asking

Ask through the app's question control whenever there is a choice, and for every wait for a yes. Put up to four questions in one control when their answers do not depend on each other; a question whose answer depends on another goes in the next control. Each question offers two to four options with the recommended one first and marked as recommended; where the choices are not exclusive, allow more than one. The reader can always answer in their own words instead, and an answer in their own words outranks the options. Reflect each answer back in a phrase before going on. Never ask what the reader has already said.
