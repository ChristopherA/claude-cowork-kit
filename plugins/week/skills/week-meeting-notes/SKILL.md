# Meeting notes

After a meeting, turn what the reader has, a cleaned transcript, the capture sheet from the meeting pack, their own memory, into one note that someone who was not there could act on: what was decided, who does what, and what the discussion settled or left open, by topic rather than in the order it was said. The note goes into the working folder, since a candid meeting is not for a synced doc; what the reader owes from it is offered to the priorities doc.

## Where this runs

This skill belongs to the week project, whose project docs are `priorities.md`. Before anything else, check that it is here. If not, this is another project: say which project this skill is for and stop, so nothing is written into the wrong folder or the wrong docs; if this is the right project and it is not set up yet, offer its setup, `set up my week project`.

## Take stock

Ask in one control, skipping what the reader has said: what the meeting was (who, when, what for, and whether it is one of a series); and what materials exist (a transcript, raw or cleaned; the capture sheet; an agenda; a summary from the meeting tool; the reader's memory). A raw transcript is cleaned first with the transcript skill; do not build notes on a noisy one. If the folder is out of reach, the note comes back in the chat for the reader to save.

Read the capture sheet or the meeting pack if there was one, so the note measures the meeting against what the reader came for. For a meeting in a series, read the last note in the series before the transcript: the transcript cannot say what did not happen, and the last note can.

## What leads

Decide what kind of meeting it was, because that decides what goes first. A review or feedback meeting leads with the verdict. A planning meeting leads with what was decided and who moved. A status meeting leads with progress and what is blocked. A first meeting or a catch-up leads with what was found in common and what follows; for a one-to-one of that kind, notes organized by person (theirs, mine, shared) are fine. Whatever the kind, the most important outcome is in the first lines; a note that buries the decision under the discussion is the failure this skill exists to prevent.

## The note

In this order, only the parts with content:

1. **Header.** Date, who was there by name, what the meeting was for, the materials it draws on.
2. **Decisions.** A short list of what was decided, each in one line, apart from the discussion so they cannot be missed.
3. **Action items.** Each with an owner and a date where one was said, as a checkbox line: the reader's own items first, other people's after. Then the topics deferred to a next meeting.
4. **What this meeting changed.** Three to five lines: what is now settled that was not, what disagreement surfaced, what opportunity or risk appeared. Not a summary of the discussion; its significance.
5. **Discussion, by topic.** Group everything said on a subject under one heading whatever the minute it was said in, a few lines each: the points, where people agreed and did not, and the quotes that carry a point, inline where they support it, never in a section of their own. Every quote is checked against this meeting's transcript before it goes in; a meeting in a series is the case where a line from the last meeting slips into this one's notes.
6. **Resources.** Links, documents and names mentioned, so they can be found again.
7. **For a series.** Two more parts, drawn from the last note and not from the transcript: the carry-forward, every action item and deferred topic from the last note that did not come up, with the date it was first raised, as checkbox lines, and a line saying so for any item three meetings old; and any standing commitment the group has made to itself, reported even when the answer is that it was not discussed, since a commitment that stops being reported has lapsed and the note is the only place that shows.

If the meeting tool produced its own summary, compare it against the transcript after the note is drafted and say in one line what it caught that the note missed and what it got wrong; do not build the note from it, and do not add such a comparison when there is nothing to compare.

Where the transcript holds candid remarks about named people, say so before showing the note, and write the discussion in neutral terms; the transcript is the record and the note is what may be shared.

## Write on a yes

Show the note in full and wait for a yes. On a yes, write it into the folder beside the meeting's other files, named by date, `YYYY-MM-DD`, and who the meeting was with, read it back, and confirm by file name. Then offer, on its own yes, the reader's own action items as lines in `priorities.md`, in the shape that doc uses; the other people's items stay in the note. If the reader means to share the note, offer a short cover message in their voice with a subject line, in plain text, handed back and never sent.

## Ending

Report the note by file name, the count of decisions and of the reader's own action items, the carry-forward count for a series, and anything flagged as candid. End with one word for completeness: full (note written), partial (written with a gap, such as an unnamed speaker or a missing transcript, with what), or minimal (stopped early).

This skill is learned from the kit author's own meeting-notes practice under Claude Code, rewritten for a task with no vault: the topic-first shape, decisions apart, quotes inline and checked against this transcript only, and the carry-forward for a series.

## Asking

Ask through the app's question control whenever there is a choice, and for every wait for a yes. Put up to four questions in one control when their answers do not depend on each other; a question whose answer depends on another goes in the next control. Each question offers two to four options with the recommended one first and marked as recommended; where the choices are not exclusive, allow more than one. The reader can always answer in their own words instead, and an answer in their own words outranks the options. Reflect each answer back in a phrase before going on. Never ask what the reader has already said. Silence is never a yes: a yes is a tap on the control or a word.

## What this skill does not do

It does not clean the transcript; the transcript skill does. It does not send the note or the cover message. It does not create a file for a person mentioned; it names them in the note. It does not write the note or change `priorities.md` without a yes.
