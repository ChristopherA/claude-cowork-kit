# Clarify

Reach a decision the reader can act on, and then stop. This skill asks; it does not build. It fits any project, and it fits the knowledge project most, where the questions are small and recur: one note or three, which thread this belongs to, whether a source deserves a note at all. It also fits the reader who has been handed a decision without the information to make it and wants options and a recommendation: most of the work then is finding what already decides it.

Two shapes. A small decision is worked one question at a time, below. A plan with many branches, a new project's scope, a reorganization of the folder, a course of study, is worked as a design tree in rounds, under "A plan, in rounds".

Facts are your job; decisions are the reader's. When a question turns on something you could find out, the folder's contents, what a project doc says, whether a note already exists, look it up and say what you found; never ask the reader for what you can read. Ask only for what is theirs to decide: what they want, what they will tolerate, which of two goods matters more.

## Before the first question

Look for what already decides this. Read `map.md`'s settled conventions and current work, the inbox if the decision is about a capture, and any note or thread the reader names. If something there already answers the question, say so and ask only what it leaves open. A dialogue that starts cold re-derives, worse, what the reader wrote down last month.

If the reader has already said what they want on one dimension, do not ask about it. Skip anything the request answered.

## One question at a time

For each open dimension of the decision, in the order that lets earlier answers shape later questions:

1. Say what is at stake in a sentence or two: what changes depending on the answer.
2. Give your recommendation and why, then the honest trade against the alternatives. Two or three options, never a list of everything possible.
3. Ask the one question, recommended option first, marked as recommended. If the choice has no clean options, ask in prose instead of forcing a menu.
4. Wait for the answer. Then reflect it back in your own words, name the assumption it rests on, and say if it changes a question you were going to ask.

If the reader answers with a question, or reframes in their own words, the framing was wrong. Do not repeat the same options. Say what you assumed, and ask again from their frame.

Never bundle two independent choices into one question; the reader cannot answer half of one.

## A plan, in rounds

When the decision is a plan with several branches, map it as a tree: every decision opens the decisions that hang off it. The frontier is every decision whose prerequisites are settled, the ones you can ask now without guessing at answers you have not heard. Ask the whole frontier in one round: number each question, give the stakes in a sentence, and give your recommended answer under it. Then wait. The reader's answers reshape the tree; recompute the frontier and ask the next round. A question whose answer depends on another still open in this round belongs to a later round. Where the app's question control cannot carry a round, ask the round in prose with the same numbering and recommendations.

The plan is done when the frontier is empty: every branch visited, nothing left silently assumed. Say so, summarize as under Ending, and act on none of it until the reader confirms you have reached a shared understanding.

This shape is learned from Matt Pocock's grill-me and grilling skills, published under the MIT license, and not copied from them.

## Every few decisions

Ask the question the loop cannot ask itself: does the premise still hold? Should this note, thread, or project exist at all? A run of consistent answers is not the same as a good decision; it is a sign nobody has re-opened the first one.

## Ending

When the dimensions are settled, or the reader stops, write a short summary of what was decided, each decision in one line with the reason, and what was left open. Do not start doing any of it. If something decided should be recorded, say where it would go (a convention in `map.md`, a line in a thread) and wait to be asked.

## What this skill does not do

It does not write notes, edit the folder, or change `map.md`. It does not decide for the reader by picking a default when they are silent. It does not ask more than one question per turn, except as a numbered round in the plan shape.
