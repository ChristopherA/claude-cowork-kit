# Clarify

Reach a decision the reader can act on, and then stop. This skill asks; it does not build. It fits any binder, and it fits the research binder most, where the questions are small and recur: one note or three, which thread this belongs to, whether a source deserves a note at all. It also fits the reader who has been handed a decision without the information to make it and wants options and a recommendation: most of the work then is finding what already decides it.

This skill runs one direction: you hold the information, or find it, and the reader decides. When it is the other way round, the reader holds what is needed and it has to be drawn out of them before anything is designed, that is the interview skill, not this one; say so and switch.

Facts are your job; decisions are the reader's. When a question turns on something you could find out, the folder's contents, what a Context document says, whether a note already exists, look it up and say what you found; never ask the reader for what you can read. Ask only for what is theirs to decide: what they want, what they will tolerate, which of two goods matters more.

## Before the first question

Look for what already decides this. Read `wrap-up.md` if one exists, then the binder's own Context documents: in the research binder `map.md`'s settled conventions and current work and the inbox if the decision is about a capture; elsewhere the priorities, mission, categories or questions document; and any note or thread the reader names. If something there already answers the question, say so and ask only what it leaves open. A dialogue that starts cold re-derives, worse, what the reader wrote down last month.

If the reader has already said what they want on one dimension, do not ask about it. Skip anything the request answered.

## One question at a time

For each open dimension of the decision, in the order that lets earlier answers shape later questions:

1. Say what is at stake in a sentence or two: what changes depending on the answer.
2. Give your recommendation and why, then the honest trade against the alternatives. Two or three options, never a list of everything possible.
3. Ask the one question, recommended option first, marked as recommended. If the choice has no clean options, ask in prose instead of forcing a menu.
4. Wait for the answer. Then reflect it back in your own words, name the assumption it rests on, and say if it changes a question you were going to ask.

If the reader answers with a question, or reframes in their own words, the framing was wrong. Do not repeat the same options. Say what you assumed, and ask again from their frame.

Never bundle two independent choices into one question; the reader cannot answer half of one.

## Every few decisions

Ask the question the loop cannot ask itself: does the premise still hold? Should this note, thread, or binder exist at all? A run of consistent answers is not the same as a good decision; it is a sign nobody has re-opened the first one.

## Ending

When the dimensions are settled, or the reader stops, write a short summary of what was decided, each decision in one line with the reason, and what was left open. Do not start doing any of it. If something decided should be recorded, say where it would go (a convention in `map.md`, a line in a thread) and wait to be asked. End with one word for completeness: full, partial (with what is missing), or minimal (stopped early).

## Asking

Ask through the app's question control whenever there is a choice, and for every wait for a yes. Put up to four questions in one control when their answers do not depend on each other; a question whose answer depends on another goes in the next control. Each question offers two to four options with the recommended one first and marked as recommended; where the choices are not exclusive, allow more than one. The reader can always answer in their own words instead, and an answer in their own words outranks the options. Reflect each answer back in a phrase before going on. Never ask what the reader has already said. Silence is never a yes: a yes is a tap on the control or a word.

## What this skill does not do

It does not write notes, edit the folder, or change `map.md`. It does not decide for the reader by picking a default when they are silent. It does not ask more than one question per dimension, and it does not interview; the interview skill does.
