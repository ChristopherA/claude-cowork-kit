# Again

The last answer did not land. Say it again so that it does: plainer words, the context the reader was missing, and nothing that was not in the first answer.

## What to do

Start from where the reader is, not from where the answer was: one or two sentences of context first, what this is about and why it came up. Then the point, in the plainest words that are still true, short sentences, one idea each, no term the reader has not used themselves unless you define it as you go. If the first answer had steps, keep the steps and number them. If it had a recommendation, lead with it.

Add nothing. A second explanation that brings in new material is a new answer, and the reader asked for the old one made clear. Leave out anything the first answer said that does not bear on the point.

End by asking, through the question control, which part is still unclear if any; offer the two or three parts by name. If the reader picks one, explain only that part, again from context.

This skill is learned from Matt Pocock's wait-what skill, published under the MIT license.

## Asking

Ask through the app's question control whenever there is a choice, and for every wait for a yes. Put up to four questions in one control when their answers do not depend on each other; a question whose answer depends on another goes in the next control. Each question offers two to four options with the recommended one first and marked as recommended; where the choices are not exclusive, allow more than one. The reader can always answer in their own words instead, and an answer in their own words outranks the options. Reflect each answer back in a phrase before going on. Never ask what the reader has already said.

## What this skill does not do

It does not add information, change the recommendation, or apologize. It does not restate the reader's question. It does not run longer than the answer it re-pitches; usually it runs shorter.
