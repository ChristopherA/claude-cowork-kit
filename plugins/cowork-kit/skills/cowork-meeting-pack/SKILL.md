# Meeting pack

A short meeting with a professional, an accountant, a lawyer, a contractor, a teacher, an advisor, goes better when three things are written beforehand for three readers: a one-page brief the professional can scan in a minute, a script the reader follows so a twenty-minute slot is not spent on preamble, and a capture sheet for whoever takes notes, so the professional's exact words survive the visit. For a clinician, the medical project's visit prep does this from the record; this is the same pack for everyone else.

## Take stock

Ask in one control what does not depend on the rest: who the meeting is with (role, and whether this is a first meeting or an established one), when, and what the reader most needs to leave with (an answer, a decision, a document, a referral, a price). Then, in a second control: whether a companion is coming, and what the reader will bring or send ahead. Read whatever the project holds on the matter first, the priorities doc, an open thread, the folder's file on this person or this matter, and do not ask for what it already says.

## The questions first

Draft the questions before any document, as the questions-for skill does: each helps the reader understand rather than putting the professional on the defensive, leads with the reader's own situation, is specific enough that a vague answer would be obviously inadequate, and carries one idea. Show them, most important first, and get a yes; the pack is built from them.

## Three documents

1. **The brief**, one page, handed over at the start or sent ahead. A header (who the reader is, the matter, who else will be there, who referred them if anyone); the reason for the meeting in one bold sentence with a line of context; anything enclosed or sent ahead; the facts the professional needs, as a table where there are several (dates, amounts, names of documents); what the reader has already done or tried; then "what I need from this meeting", the questions as numbered asks; and only after that the history, which may run over. The asks stay on page one.
2. **The script**, for the reader, read before and glanced at during. One sentence at the top saying who this person is and what the reader's job is in the room. On arrival: hand over the brief; if the reader means to record, ask first, since the rules differ from place to place. An opening statement of three or four sentences, quoted, that says what has changed since the last contact if there was one. Then the questions, each with a listen-for line and one follow-up if the answer leaves a gap. Then "who do you know": the professional's own network for anything the reader is still looking for. Last, "if time runs short": what must be settled before leaving and what can follow by email.
3. **The capture sheet**, for the companion or for the reader afterwards. Before the meeting: the brief printed or sent, the enclosures packed. One section per question with room for the exact words and a line for the outcome. A section for names and contacts of anyone referred. Follow-up: what was agreed, what the professional will do and by when, what the reader will do, the next contact. A few lines to fill in before leaving: the main outcome, whether the reader got what they came for, the most urgent next step, anything missed.

Where a point is contested, a fee the reader questions, a decision they were told and doubt, a matter with another party behind it, the question leads with the reader's own history, and the capture sheet asks for the exact words on it.

## Write on a yes

Name the three files by date and role, such as `2026-10-02 accountant brief.md`, and write them into the folder on the matter, each on its own yes, read back and confirmed by file name; where there is no folder, hand them back in the chat. If the reader wants to print or send the brief, produce a document version, kept to one page.

## Ending

Report the three files by name and the number of asks on page one. End with one word for completeness: full (three documents written), partial (some written, with what is missing), or minimal (stopped early). Say that the capture sheet comes back to the project after the meeting, for the notes.

This skill is learned from the kit author's own appointment-preparation practice, generalized to any professional and rewritten with nothing of that situation in it.

## What this skill does not do

It does not book or change the meeting, send anything, or contact the professional. It does not answer the questions itself. It does not write without a yes.

## Asking

Ask through the app's question control whenever there is a choice, and for every wait for a yes. Put up to four questions in one control when their answers do not depend on each other; a question whose answer depends on another goes in the next control. Each question offers two to four options with the recommended one first and marked as recommended; where the choices are not exclusive, allow more than one. The reader can always answer in their own words instead, and an answer in their own words outranks the options. Reflect each answer back in a phrase before going on. Never ask what the reader has already said.
